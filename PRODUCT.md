# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

- **The child, 4–12, plays alone** for 10–15 minutes a day on a parent's phone or their own. Children of 4–6 do not read yet: they play by pictures and by ear. Children of 7–12 read, keep streaks, ranks and seasons, and compete.
- **A grown-up sets it up**: the family, the sounds to practise, the languages, the book being read. They look at progress and sometimes play together with the child.
- **Relatives far away** (grandparents and other family) join through a family link: they see the children's successes and play some games with them.
- **Friend families** meet in a family league, a weekly shared goal and online chess.

## Product Purpose

Daily speech practice (the hard sounds Р, Л, Ш, С and others), reading and maths, plus a wide world to discover: a museum of paintings, composers, travel, playground physics. All of it sits in one game that a child wants to come back to every day.

Success means:
- the child practises every day on their own;
- the hard sounds get set and then hold in everyday speech;
- the child reads more;
- relatives see the progress and take part, even from far away.

## Positioning

- **Speech therapy by ear**: the phone listens to how the child says sounds and words, and the friend answers by voice.
- **The whole family is inside the game**, not only the child.
- **Built for bilingual children**: the game runs in Russian, English, Portuguese and German, and uses every language the child speaks.

## Operating Context

- A phone, mostly an iPhone, added to the home screen. Played at home, on the road, before bed.
- Sessions are short. The younger child often plays with no grown-up reading the screen.
- The internet may be slow or partly blocked where relatives live, so the game keeps working offline once loaded.
- Voices come from the phone or from cloud voices.
- A family signs in with a word and a 4-picture code. Profiles sync through the family server.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- One source game in Russian, with builds in English, Portuguese and German. A game that cannot work in a language is hidden there.
- Speech recognition and text-to-speech run in the browser.
- Data stays on the phone and on the family server.
- The author sees anonymous summaries. Names are added only with the parents' tick. Letters to the author are encrypted for her phone.
- Photos of toys never leave the phone.
- Online chess with strangers: no names, no ages, no chat, and a grown-up allows it.
- Book content: tools around the child's own book, never the book's text.
- No copies of existing commercial games.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: «Говорилки» (with the friend Рыжик).
- The child's friend (a tiger, kitten and others) is the one who talks. The screen does not repeat in print what the friend says.
- Russian voice must be correct: stress, ё, numbers, and names declined in sentences.
- Marketing and content stay warm, short and concrete.

## Evidence on Hand

- Daily use by the author's family.
- No testimonials, ratings or usage numbers to quote. Never invent them.

## Product Principles

1. **A child who cannot read can play everything**: pictures, the friend's voice, and buttons that read themselves aloud.
2. **The friend speaks, the screen shows**: no text printed twice, no small print for little ones.
3. **Ten minutes, every day**: a clear next step on the home screen, short rounds, rewards for coming back rather than for grinding.
4. **The family is part of the game, privately**: relatives join easily, names and photos stay at home unless the grown-ups say otherwise.
5. **Every language is first-class**: what a child sees in English, Portuguese or German is as complete and correct as in Russian.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Tap targets sized for small fingers.
- Everything important is voiced for non-readers.
- Readable contrast in both the day and the night theme.
- Works on older iPhones and on slow networks.
